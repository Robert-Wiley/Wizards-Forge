# Break This

This is an invitation.

If you think part of Governance Physics is wrong, incomplete, overfit, domain-specific, mathematically weak, structurally misleading, or impossible to reproduce:

**show us.**

## Good ways to attack the work

- Apply the GRC topology to a decentralized organization.
- Test whether relationship types behave differently across industries.
- Identify nodes that should not be universal.
- Find missing governance, risk, or compliance primitives.
- Show that a propagation assumption fails under sparse evidence.
- Compare centralized and federated authority structures.
- Test whether confidence and strength are being conflated.
- Challenge the topology with healthcare, finance, higher education, defense, energy, AI systems, or critical infrastructure.
- Reproduce an experiment and report a different result.
- Propose a competing mathematical formulation.
- Demonstrate a case where the graph structure hides a real-world dependency.
- Find a relationship that is directionally wrong.
- Show where temporal change invalidates a static interpretation.

## First open challenge

### Decentralized Organization Topology

**Question:** How does organizational centralization versus decentralization alter the structure, propagation behavior, and assurance characteristics of a GRC topology?

Potential observations include:
- seam density,
- bridge concentration,
- path length,
- clustering,
- duplicated authority,
- fragmented accountability,
- propagation chains,
- isolated local governance clusters,
- escalation latency,
- and assurance degradation.

There is no preferred outcome. A result that breaks the current model is useful.

## What to include when challenging a claim

When practical, provide:
1. the object, relationship, formula, or assumption being challenged;
2. the environment or domain;
3. the evidence or observation;
4. the expected behavior;
5. the observed behavior;
6. a proposed revision, if one exists.

**“This does not work here” is a valid starting point.**
