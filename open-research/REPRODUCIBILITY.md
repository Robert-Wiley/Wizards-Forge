# Reproducibility

Governance Physics should increasingly be reproducible by people who were not present when the ideas were created.

## Minimum reproducibility target

For a published experiment or analytical claim, aim to preserve:
- version,
- research question,
- input data or a publishable surrogate,
- schema,
- assumptions,
- relationship definitions,
- transformation steps,
- code or pseudocode where appropriate,
- expected output,
- observed output,
- and known limitations.

## Cross-system research pattern

**Atlas hypothesis → reproducible implementation → protected validation → promoted finding**

Different environments may hold different layers, but public claims should expose enough information for meaningful independent testing whenever possible.

## Negative results

Failed reproductions and null findings belong in the record.

They should not be discarded merely because they weaken a preferred interpretation.

## Versioning

A result should identify the research version it tested. The topology is expected to change over time; a finding against v0.1 may not apply to v0.5.
